# Forward Passes, Backward Passes, and Fumbles

## Legal forward passes

The offense may throw one forward pass during a down, and the pass must be released from behind the
line of scrimmage. Once the ball has crossed the line of scrimmage, the offense cannot bring it back
behind the line and then throw a forward pass.

A pass is illegal when it is a second forward pass, is released after a change of possession, or is
released when the passer's entire body and the ball are beyond the line of scrimmage. The exact
penalty depends on which restriction was violated.

## Forward movement and loss of control

An intentional forward movement of the passer's hand begins a forward pass. If contact causes the
passer to lose the ball after that movement begins, the play is treated as a pass. If the passer loses
the ball while pulling it back toward the body to re-cock the arm, it is a fumble.

## Backward passes

A pass is backward when it first touches a player or the ground at a yard line parallel to or behind
the point where it left the passer's hand. A backward pass that hits the ground remains live and may
be recovered and advanced by either team. A snap is a backward pass.

## Fumbles

A fumble is a loss of player possession that is not a pass or kick. The ball ordinarily remains live,
and either team may recover it. Special restrictions govern which offensive player may advance a
fumble on fourth down or after the two-minute warning, so a ruling in either situation requires those
additional facts.

## Intentional grounding

Intentional grounding can occur when a passer facing an imminent loss of yardage throws a forward
pass without a realistic eligible receiver in its direction and vicinity. A passer who has been
outside the pocket avoids grounding by throwing the ball to or beyond the line of scrimmage, even if
no receiver has a realistic chance to catch it. Other exceptions and timing rules may affect the
ruling, so the passer's location, pressure, target, and landing point all matter.
