# Completing a Catch

## Elements of a completed catch

For a forward pass to be complete, an inbounds player must first control the ball before it touches
the ground. The player must then touch the ground inbounds with both feet, or with another body part
other than the hands. After those requirements, the player must perform an act common to the game or
maintain control long enough to perform one.

Examples of a football act include taking an additional step, tucking the ball and turning upfield,
extending the ball, or avoiding an opponent.

## Going to the ground

If the player has control and gets two feet or another qualifying body part down but has not yet
completed a football act, losing control when contacting the ground makes the pass incomplete. The
player does not need to keep control through the ground after all elements of the catch were already
completed; a later loss can instead be a fumble.

The ball may touch the ground without forcing an incompletion when the player maintains control and
the ground does not cause the loss of control.

## Sideline catches

A player can complete a catch at the sideline by establishing control and getting both feet inbounds
before touching out of bounds, followed by the required football act or enough time to perform one.
A toe drag can establish an inbounds foot. If a continuous heel-to-toe or toe-to-heel step places any
part of that same foot out of bounds, that foot is out of bounds.
